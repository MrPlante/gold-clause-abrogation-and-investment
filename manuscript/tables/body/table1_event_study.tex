\begin{minipage}{0.95\textwidth}\footnotesize \textit{Notes.} This table reports cumulative raw returns---the compound product of daily CRSP returns over each event window---of two equal-weighted portfolios: firms with gold-clause exposure ($\tilde{d}_j>0$) and firms without ($\tilde{d}_j=0$), where $\tilde{d}_j$ is the frozen exposure measure defined in equation~(\ref{eq:tilde_d}). Portfolios are rebalanced daily over firms with a return on each day. Within each panel, rows report the full sample (``Pooled'') and size terciles formed on market capitalization at the end of 1932, before the litigation events (gold/non-gold firm counts: pooled 177/376, small 69/116, medium 51/133, large 57/127). ``Diff.''\ is the gold-minus-non-gold differential in percentage points. The beta-adjusted differential subtracts $(\beta_G-\beta_N)\times R_m$, where $R_m$ is the value-weighted market return over the window and portfolio betas are equal-weighted averages of firm-level market betas estimated from daily returns over the full 1926--1940 sample (pooled: $\beta_G=1.01$, $\beta_N=0.90$; small: $\beta_G=0.93$, $\beta_N=0.90$; medium: $\beta_G=1.11$, $\beta_N=0.91$; large: $\beta_G=1.02$, $\beta_N=0.90$). $t$-statistics (in parentheses) scale each differential by $\hat\sigma\sqrt{h}$, where $h$ is the number of trading days in the window and $\hat\sigma$ is the standard deviation of the corresponding daily differential over the pre-abrogation period (July 1926--December 1932). For the Supreme Court decision (February~18, 1935), the benchmark is the close of February~16---the last trading day before the ruling, as the exchange was open on Saturdays. The November window combines the certiorari grant in \textit{United States v.\ Bankers Trust Co.} (Monday, November~5) with the midterm election (Tuesday, November~6, for which the exchange was closed). Internet Appendix Section~\ref{sec:ia_other_events} examines the intermediate legal events of the litigation period in the same format.\end{minipage}
